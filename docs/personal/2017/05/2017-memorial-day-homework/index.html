2017 Memorial Day Homework

I have made the formal household declaration that all screen time will be matched, hour-for-hour with project time.

Yesterday we cleaned the 1st floor, mopped and everything. Today we went grocery shopping, made lunch, and then started on homework time.

To say that I am unpopular here, would be an understatement. But both of our kids will benefit from some extra assignments. Our education system is super lax, and a bit of supplemental assignment seems like a good idea.

The Screen Time Equation

The rule is simple: For every hour of screens (TV, iPad, games), they need to do an hour of productive project work. This includes:

  • Academic work: Math practice, reading, writing
  • Life skills: Cooking, cleaning, organizing
  • Creative projects: Art, building, music
  • Physical activity: Sports, bike riding, hiking

Why This Matters

I’ve noticed that unlimited screen time turns them into zombies. They lose interest in everything else - books, outdoor play, building with LEGOs, even talking to us at dinner.

The 1:1 ratio forces them to be intentional about screen time. If they really want to watch that show or play that game, they need to earn it.

Early Results

Day 1: Massive resistance. Lots of complaints about how “unfair” this is.

Day 2: Started negotiating. “Can we do the dishes AND vacuum to get extra screen time?”

Day 3: Actually asked if they could read for an hour to “bank” screen time for later.

The shift in thinking is already happening.

Learning Moments

What I didn’t expect was how much I would learn from this:

  • They’re actually capable of way more than I give them credit for
  • When they’re not overstimulated by screens, they find creative ways to entertain themselves
  • They take pride in completing tasks when there’s a clear reward structure

The Long Game

This isn’t about being anti-technology. I work in tech, and screens are part of modern life. But I want them to understand that consumption needs to be balanced with creation.

The goal is to build habits now that will serve them as adults:

  • Time management
  • Delayed gratification
  • Finding satisfaction in productive work
  • Appreciating entertainment more when it’s earned

Will report back on how this experiment goes over the summer…
